About

Hi! I'm Elvira Al Bataineh. I study how people make high-stakes security decisions with AI: when analysts and policymakers should trust an AI system's assessment, who is accountable when they act on it, and how to design tools that keep humans meaningfully in charge.

I am a Graduate Research Fellow at Norwich University, where I am completing graduate thesis research with Dr. Diane M. Zorri. I hold an M.S. in Cybersecurity (Artificial Intelligence) and an M.A. in Diplomacy (Cyber Diplomacy) from Norwich University, and a B.S. in Cybersecurity, summa cum laude.

I am seeking a PhD in human–computer interaction, focused on human oversight and accountability in AI-assisted decision-making.

Research

My current work centers on the AIS-Index (AI-Informed Incident Severity Index), a decision-support framework for assessing AI-enabled cyber incidents, paired with an Early Warning System for allied coordination. It addresses what I call the Detection–Governance Gap: AI can flag threats at machine speed, but the people and institutions who must decide what to do move far more slowly.

I am now bringing human–computer interaction methods into this work, to study how decision-support tools like the AIS-Index shape decision accuracy, trust calibration, and bias.
